MAIN DECISION

Separate whether a requested action was committed from what control proceeds
next. Neither is a question about net hand size, unit count or a fingerprint.
An action can be applied and then countered, lose its targets, or have its
effects offset. Ending a held move is not applying the ability chosen before it.

The move defect also requires separating two meanings currently hidden inside
pending_move: an unfinished selection of a simultaneous group, and an already
completed move whose showdown is waiting. A valid reaction window is not a
license to resume choosing members of an unfinished Standard Move.

1. THE RULES ANSWER: WHICH WINDOW ACTUALLY EXISTS?
================================================

1.1 A Standard Move is one action, including all selected movers

Rules 144.1.b–c prohibit a Standard Move during a Closed State, Showdown or
Combat. Rule 144.3 treats a simultaneous multi-unit Standard Move as one game
action; 144.3.a gives the common destination and 144.3.c requires simultaneous
exhaustion costs. Rules 421.3.a–b identify the cost and movement effect.

Consequently, the player chooses the complete group before processing its
movement-trigger reaction windows. They cannot move Merchant, inspect its
discard/draw chain and the opponent's responses, then decide that another unit
was part of the same simultaneous move. That is information unavailable when
the single group action was declared. H: rules interpretation. The current
sequential-MOVE interface makes this distinction real, not merely terminology.

Rules 333 and 334.1–2 require completing outstanding tasks before ordinary
FEPR processing. A move causes a Cleanup (319.8, 455). Its triggers become
pending chain items (383.3); staging and opening a showdown are different
steps (323.7–12). In particular, 323.11–12 and 344 require Neutral Open State
to begin a new showdown/combat. An existing move-trigger chain is Closed State:
the engine must not open the owed showdown inside it.

| Open showdown after the move | Yes, if the source is usable; these three are Add Reactions. 313.1–4, 347.1, 430.2. | Now. They do not end the move again. | The current showdown continues; do not restart it or discard another battlefield's staged showdown. |

The second row is an important exception to any blanket menu ban. Rule 430.3
even gives an example of moving two units into Mageseeker Investigator's tax:
Add Reactions may be used despite the absence of a priority window. Kai'Sa's
added power is spell-only; permission to activate an Add does not make those
resources legal payment for a movement tax. Keep spending restrictions intact.

Gold, Seal of Discord and Kai'Sa all finalize and resolve their Add immediately
(337.2, 430.2). Their Add does not itself pass priority or focus (430.2.a).
Other pending triggered items can still be finalized and determine the next
priority holder under 337.4. Retaining focus and receiving the next priority
decision are therefore separate facts.

1.3 What is wrong with simply removing the ability from legal_actions?

It hides the valid action in real reaction/showdown windows, and violates the
payment exception. It also leaves the erroneous showdown opening/discharge in
place for other action kinds. Conversely, changing noop to acted after clearing
pending_move acknowledges a transition but still never activates the source.
Neither is the fix. H: current code plus the reproductions.

At loop.py:5500–5503, the generic dispatcher ends any pending move and returns
before activating the selected ability. Its tail at 5695–5696 also flushes a
pending move for non-MOVE actions. Both are main-declaration policy embedded in
a dispatcher reused by reaction/showdown execution.

_flush_pending_move clears the only marker before calling _run_showdown.
_run_showdown then refuses an already active showdown (7741–7742), losing the
marker, or can open one despite an existing chain. The helper has no chain-empty
guard. This is why the design must repair ownership of the continuation,
not merely the Boolean returned by _use_ability_now.

1.4 Minimal state model and recommended migration boundary

Use distinct concepts, with explicit ownership:

- MoveDraft: declaring side, common destination and selected movers with their
  origins. No reaction opportunity is exposed to uncommitted movement effects.
- CommittedMove: all legality/payment decisions have succeeded; the cohort is
  fixed, its exhaustion/movement happens as one operation, then its triggers
  and cleanup work are processed.
- StagedShowdown: battlefield, contesting side and which contest/obligation it
  belongs to. Reuse/enrich the existing staged_showdowns queue rather than
  inventing a second independent scheduler.
- Chain/Showdown frame: current priority/focus and the cause of its opening.
  A staged showdown is consumed when that specific showdown starts, not when
  any ability is attempted.

Canonical state should have no open MoveDraft inside a normal reaction or
showdown window. It can have a suspended payment task. Copies must preserve
these contexts; a fresh GameLoop(gs.clone()) does not currently preserve all
loop-local pending work. This design does not claim a DTO alone fixes that.

Keep existing tuple spellings and private entry points as adapters during
migration. Internally route main, reaction, showdown and payment contexts
explicitly; the main-only move-declaration barrier must not run in the other
three. Remove both unconditional pending-move flushes from the shared action
body. Let the owner of move commitment/cleanup schedule the owed showdown.

The group-selection UI/AI boundary needs an explicit choice before commitment:
either a batch of mover references or a draft-selection decision. Legacy scalar
MOVE is sufficient to express a single-unit move, not an entire unspecified
simultaneous group. Do not silently default to moving every ready unit, or let
old heuristic code select more movers after seeing the triggers. Migrate the
random/simple_trade group-selection policy deliberately, preserving its RNG
stream and menu order where the rules permit. Golden changes from this policy
boundary must be isolated from the outcome-contract change.

This atomic-group correction is a separate rules/timing commit. It is not an
eight-line mechanical outcome migration. Tests observing partially moved groups
must move their assertions to the explicit commit boundary; maintaining old
private names cannot preserve an incorrect intermediate-timing contract.

Compatibility for a legacy state containing pending_move inside a window:
first identify whether it means an uncommitted draft, an owed future showdown,
or the showdown already being serviced. Do not erase it unconditionally. A
different battlefield remains queued; the current one is not reopened. If the
contesting side has lost all units, re-evaluate 323.7.a, 323.9–10 rather than
guaranteeing a showdown after every original move. Never nest a new showdown
while another is ongoing (462).

Confidence M in this state/adapter design; H that the existing single marker
does not distinguish these cases. Full atomic payment/finalization rollback is
not implemented or established by the review. Validate before committing and
apply 358.5 where the process of play requires undo, rather than assuming an
outcome enum supplies transactionality.

2. EXPLICIT OUTCOME CONTRACT
============================

2.1 The result describes the request, not net state change

Design vocabulary, not an implementation:

Application = APPLIED | REJECTED | ENDED_PENDING_MOVE | PASSED

APPLIED:
  The requested legal play/activation/move/hide crossed its commitment point.
  Its resolution may subsequently be countered, mistarget, do nothing or have
  offsetting effects. 359.3.e.1 explicitly permits resolution with illegal
  targets. Do not redefine application as effect success.

REJECTED:
  The request did not cross that point: wrong window, invalid source/destination,
  unavailable targets/costs, etc. Return an explicit reason. Admission rejection
  does not pay the requested action's costs. Do not label a partial execution or
  swallowed exception a clean rejection. Independently authorized Add payments
  are separate committed operations and need their own accounting/undo rules.

ENDED_PENDING_MOVE:
  The main-phase adapter committed/ended the held move instead of executing the
  newly proposed action. The proposed card/ability remains unplayed/unspent.
  Drain its legitimate continuations and obtain a fresh decision. Do not treat
  this as success in a resource-gain probe or as a pass of chain priority.

PASSED:
  A deliberate pass in the current window. Main PASS ending a move is mapped to
  ENDED_PENDING_MOVE. Main PASS without one ends acting; reaction PASS passes
  priority; showdown PASS passes focus. These are different continuations.

Group member selection is a draft choice, not a committed Standard Move. Its
choice acknowledgment should not be falsely reported as a completed rules
action. Produce the move action's APPLIED outcome at group commitment.

Separate application from control disposition:

Priority policy:
  KEEP_CURRENT / TO_CONTROLLER_OF_NEXT_FINALIZED_ITEM / PASS_TO_NEXT / NONE
Focus policy:
  KEEP_CURRENT / PASS_AFTER_THIS_CHAIN_CLOSES / PASS_NOW / NONE

The window driver owns the actual priority/focus frame. The result can carry
the actor, action kind/source reference, explicit commitment/finalization
receipt, chain-opening cause and rejection reason. A terminal game result is
orthogonal; a valid action can win immediately.

Record the commitment fact before calling anything that can resolve a chain or
flush triggers, rather than deciding it after that call returns. Nested actions
produce separate receipts; never use a mutable global last_action_succeeded
flag. A replay _Stop can intentionally unwind after commitment but before the
outer method returns. Its continuation/frame must still retain the committed
fact. Do not catch that BaseException control signal as a rejection.

Do not make the new object implicitly truthy. In design, __bool__ should reject
implicit conversion, or the API must otherwise enforce an explicit status test.
The ordinary truthiness of a dataclass would make REJECTED truthy in the old
base_agent code. Legacy Boolean/string wrappers must map the enum explicitly.

No global rules-state fingerprint is part of this contract. Keep an independent
semantic snapshot for tests/diagnostics and a separate bounded-attempt guard for
a broken agent. APPLIED does not have to imply a net observable difference;
similarly, PASSED and ENDED_PENDING_MOVE can cause real model transitions.

2.2 Control rules the result must preserve

| Committed request | Priority | Focus / pass sequence |
|---|---|---|
| Add ability | Resolves immediately; does not itself pass priority. | Does not itself pass focus. Any prior consecutive-pass streak is broken by the added item/action. |
| Non-Add activated ability or spell | Finalize, then grant priority to the controller of the next finalized item (337.1.a, 337.4). This is often the same player, not simply the opponent. | If it opened an ordinary player-action chain in a showdown, pass focus only when that chain closes (340.2.a, 346–347). |
| Unit or gear | Immediate resolution after finalization (337.2). In an existing chain, subsequent priority follows the next finalized item. | In a showdown this is an ordinary played-card continuation: transfer focus once its play/ordinary chain has completed. Trigger subchains must not create an extra transfer or erase the original transfer obligation. Net bodies/cards are irrelevant. |
| Trigger-origin or Add-origin chain closes | Process outstanding tasks and next eligible items. | Keep focus under 346.1. A reaction added later does not change the cause that opened the chain. |
| Explicit PASS | Pass priority or focus as appropriate. | Count the pass for the current window. Two legitimate consecutive passes close/resolve it under 339.1 or 348. |
| REJECTED | No rules-level pass occurred. | Retry or apply an explicit agent error policy; the outcome must not pretend the player passed. |

Important additional live defect found while designing this contract:
_run_chain's kept branch at 7606–7607 does not reset passes. In a real open
chain: B passes; A uses Gold; A passes. Current callbacks are B,A,A and the chain
resolves. The correct opportunity sequence includes B again: B,A,A,B. Rule
339.1 requires a sequence of passes without another item being added; Add
abilities finalize/resolve an item immediately (337.2, 430.2), rather than
preserving an old pass streak. Rule 348 makes the analogous showdown condition
explicitly depend on no intervening card play or ability activation.

The original showdown kept branch already resets passes. Make both drivers
consistent from the explicit finalized/application receipt, not from net stack
growth. H: callback reproduction and rule interpretation. This pass-accounting
change is a named behavioral fix, separate from merely introducing a result type.

Pending triggers are another reason not to equate KEEP with 'the same player
will necessarily be the very next decider'. Give _flush_triggers an ordered
finalization receipt: which items were finalized, which were immediate Adds,
and which remain for FEPR. The driver applies 337.4 after handling those tasks.
The requested Add still does not itself transfer focus or priority.

For copying/replay, the continuation needs explicit clone-preserved data:
window kind, priority holder, consecutive passes, chain-opening cause and the
focus transfer owed on close, alongside draft/payment/staged-showdown state.
Move the current two loops' local pass/holder values into a small WindowFrame
on the copied graph (or equivalent copy-preserved continuation), while keeping
the loops and initialization rules intact. Reuse existing focus fields through
aliases rather than maintaining two writable authorities. Scope nested frames
and restore the suspended parent explicitly. This is a bounded control-state
extraction, not a promise that the existing bare-state clone is a full snapshot.
Reading the controller of the actual next chain item is legitimate rules
logic; guessing which operation happened from its net length is the defect.

The existing _as_task/_as_step decorators already return the wrapped method's
value. Preserve their trigger-flush points in the contract-only migration.
When fixing move timing, change those points explicitly and test the event
sequence; do not assume task-depth alone precisely represents every rules task.

For rejected actions, the first migration can retain an explicit legacy
bot-error fallback that submits a separate PASS, with a bounded attempt count,
to avoid infinite simulations. That adapter policy must be visible in tests
and receipts. A human/remote stale request should receive rejection and a fresh
menu for the same window. Do not hide a policy fallback in REJECTED itself.

Unexpected handler exceptions are not ordinary admission failures. This design
does not solve the existing partial-mutation/exception-resilience issue by
renaming it REJECTED, and it does not require rewriting every effect handler to
return a success Boolean. Most handlers can continue returning None.

3.2 Correct repair boundary for these related defects

Do not turn all registry handlers into action-status producers. Add narrow
operation receipts at authoritative points already present in the engine:

- _route_deaths knows truly_dead after death replacements. Return immutable
  actual-death facts (with subject/source/operation attribution), rather than
  making callers discover deaths from the post-resolution board.
- EffectContext.damage_units aggregates those facts across locations. A target
  banished instead produces a leave/banish fact, not a kill fact; a unit saved
  by Guardian Angel produces a recall plus the gear's death, not the unit's.
- _deal_damage's reflexive draw and _unit_split_damage's XP use actual kills
  attributed to their instruction/source, restricted to the relevant victims.
  An unrelated death triggered by the same overall action is not automatically
  a kill by that damage instruction.
- A death followed by resurrection still counts as an actual death. A final
  presence test would miss it; a receipt from the death-routing decision does
  not. Keep batching, replacement order and trigger timing unchanged while
  propagating these facts. Source/kind attribution for unit-dealt damage needs
  its own tests; do not opportunistically change it in the acknowledgment PR.
- Recorder and coach consume actual kill/draw/discard/burn/recall facts at all
  turn and resolution boundaries, including events outside discretionary
  actions. Keep snapshot diffs for turn/score/control display. They are useful
  observations, not a reconstruction of event causality.
- Redact opponent draw identities before delivering events to the coach; use
  counts for hidden draws. Existing opp_discards already solves part of that
  one case, not same-name cancellation in general.

This needs a bounded event callback/receipt collector, not an event-sourcing
rewrite or a new persistent log as the rules authority. If an old snapshot has
no causal facts, render a neutral zone change rather than inventing a death,
discard or burn. Correct narration/recording does not alter simulator outcomes

5. WHICH OF THE 28 GOLDENS SHOULD CHANGE?

5.1 Measured bounded counterfactuals — no production fix assumed

I ran all 28 existing signatures unchanged, with non-invasive method-level
observations, then four separately labeled in-memory diagnostic variants:

A. Bypass the main pending-move intercept only inside an existing chain or
   showdown, preserving the marker afterward. This isolates ability swallowing;
   it is NOT a full atomic-move or staged-showdown implementation.
B. Reset the chain pass streak before continuing after kept.
C. Treat a main pending-move completion inside _use_ability_now as not being an
   applied activation for the base-agent Boolean probe.
D. Combine A+B+C.

All five sets, including the baseline, matched 28/28 existing terminal
signatures. No fixture was regenerated. This is 140 signature runs for the
retained experiment, not a claim that the complete unimplemented design has
been tested. Repeated diagnostic development runs are not pooled into counts.

The baseline observed 122 live pending-move interceptions: 49 UNIT, 38 ABILITY,
16 SPELL, 12 GEAR and seven non-joining MOVE requests. All were main-context
compatibility completions, not the nested-window failure. There were zero
observed live nested pending-ability interceptions among these 28 chosen
trajectories. Do not misreport those 38 main ABILITY requests as 38 swallowed
legal reactions. Three additional interceptions occurred in copied main-phase
Add probes; all originally returned True for a move completion. Variant C
changed that result without changing the observed terminal signatures.

6. PHASES, RISKS AND WHAT I WOULD NOT DO
=======================================

| Phase | Change | Principal risk / gate |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Preserve baselines; write the failing acceptance tests and negative controls. | A circular fingerprint oracle or an intrusive observer hiding the failure. |

Do not split the 8,448-line loop at the same time, rename all private methods,
rewrite the registry, invent a general card DSL, repair all identity lifetimes,
