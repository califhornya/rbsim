  A Sonnet-5 parse pass (`--only-empty`, commit `29da7bb`) took the corpus from

  failures with a stronger model (e.g. Opus) will NOT help — the model is
  constrained to the allowed vocab, so it flags the same cards. The lever is
  **adding the missing primitives, then re-parsing the cards that need them.**
  Highest-leverage missing primitives (live counts from `scripts/suggested_vocab.txt`):
  `effect:gain_power_any_domain` (10), `effect:mode_choice` (9, modal "choose one"),

  (3), `effect:reveal_top_n_banish_play` (3), then a long one-off tail. Each new
  primitive = engine_vocab entry + handler/condition + a targeted re-parse of the
  cards that need it (a primitive alone flips nothing until those cards are re-parsed).
  Turnkey for a future parse (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; not available in dev/CI):
  ```
  export ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=...            # required by generate_effects.py
  RBSIM_PARSER_MODEL=claude-sonnet-5 \
    uv run python scripts/generate_effects.py --retry-review   # reprocess flagged
  uv run python scripts/coverage_audit.py      # re-measure; eyeball COVERAGE_REPORT.md
  RBSIM_REGEN_GOLDEN=1 uv run pytest tests/test_golden_games.py -q  # if gameplay changed
  uv run pytest -q                             # must stay green
  ```
  Note on cost/model: Sonnet-5 parsed the ~209-card empty tail for ≈ $2.3. A full
  Opus pass on the same set is ~5× (≈ $9+), and the evidence above says it buys
  little — reserve Opus (if ever) for a small, curated set of cards you have a
  specific reason to believe are model-misses rather than vocab gaps. Prioritize
  any card that enters a meta deck.

## Stage 0.5 (match layer)
Done (2026-10-25): `riftbound/core/match.py` follows Core Rules §488 and the
Tournament Rules (excerpt in `TOURNAMENT_RULES.md`): a played battlefield is out
for the match, best-of-5 reuse, "play first or last" as a decision, sideboarding
(deck JSON `sideboard` ≤10 + per-matchup `sideboard_plans`, champion change, never
before game 1), and the time limit as a match clock: 60 minutes per best of 3,
converted to 45 turns at an estimated 1⅓ minutes per turn. When it runs out, +3
turns, then a 2-point lead wins the game or it's a draw, and the match ends.
Riftbound has no turn limit; the per-game `max_turns` is a stall guard that counts
as the time running out. Left:
- **Recalibrate the clock** (`MINUTES_PER_TURN` in `core/match.py`) when real turn
  counts or game durations are known. With it, `simple_trade` mirrors over all repo
  decks (280 matches): 11 of 44 game-3s go to time; 71 matches end by time, most
  of them from AI stalls hitting the 40-turn guard.
- [DONE 2026-10-25] **AI stalls.** Were 53 of 420 `simple_trade` mirror games
  (12.6%) at the 40-turn guard; 0 of 420 after token units could move and fight
  and the duplicate movement handlers were removed.
- **Learned sideboarding.** Today's policies are "keep the registered list" and
  "follow the deck's fixed plans"; a net-guided policy (like the battlefield
  chooser in `ai/pregame.py`) is future work.
- **Richer battlefield chooser input.** The chooser sees its own available
  battlefields only, not the opponent's remaining ones or the match history.
- **Best-of-1 setup variant** (TR 406.1.h): not modeled.
- **Single games** (CLI, self-play, RL env) still score the 40-turn guard as "any
  lead wins"; only matches apply the time-limit rule.

## Engine choices (branch `engine-choices`, 2026-10-26)
The choices the rules give players inside effects, costs and combat are made by
the engine with fixed baseline policies (the agents and the RL action space only
pick top-level actions). Every one now goes through `GameLoop.choose` /
`choose_many` (`kind`, side, options, the baseline's pick): targets, modes, "you
may"s (effects, ACCELERATE, SHOW OFF, "you may" additional costs), the unit
combat damage kills first, the unit an Equipment attaches to, the unit / gear /
card a cost takes, cost extras, the Power domain, dig / predict, effect moves and
their destination, deploy destination. No chooser: the baseline decides.
`scripts/choice_audit.py` (report: `CHOICE_AUDIT.md`) plays random corpus decks
and replays sampled choices with each alternative forced to measure the baselines.
- [DONE] **Fixed baselines** (they lost to a random pick): "return a unit to
  hand" and "-N Might to a unit" aim at an enemy first (Rebuke, Gust, Frigid
  Touch...; they bounced / shrank our own unit); costs give up a token, then a
  unit in base, then the weakest (it was the first unit on a battlefield, and it
  went to the trash without dying: now DEATHKNELL, tokens cease to exist);
  modes are tried on a copy of the game and the best position wins
  (`effects._best_mode`, `_position_value`; it was always the first mode;
  `Agent.decide_mode` now returns None for "no preference"); combat damage kills
  the most Might it can (`combat.kill_order`; it was list order); ready / buff /
  heal skip units they would do nothing to.
- [DONE] **Data:** Sacrifice needs a Mighty unit (`unit_filter`, and is
  unplayable without one: mandatory additional costs in `_play_forbidden`),
  Zaun Punk kills a friendly gear (`kill_friendly_gear`; it killed a unit), Bard
  Mercurial exhausts the legend (`exhaust_legend`), Detonation Amplifier and

  value-equality (#25), effect-made Bomb tokens were the registry spec (a "return

## Declared choices (§355, 2026-10-27)
Before, a spell chose its targets as it resolved; the coach couldn't see what an
opponent's spell aimed at, and a unit moved in reaction just made the spell hit
another one. Now (`GameLoop._put_on_chain` / `_declare_choices`, tests in
tests/test_declared_targets.py):
- [DONE] As a spell goes on the chain its resolution runs on a deep copy of the
  game, its player choosing as usual (the chooser: coach, search); the choices
  that are targets, modes, move destinations or players are kept on the
  ChainItem (`declared`, mapped back to the real objects). As it resolves they
  are replayed; a declared option no longer among the legal ones raises
  `_Ignored` and that instruction (and an "it" after it) isn't followed
  (§359.3.e); "up to N" keeps its legal ones. Other choices (a discard from the
  hand, cards looked at in the deck, "you may") stay at resolution.
- [DONE] §355.8 / §358: a spell is offered (legal_actions) and played (the
  engine refuses it too) only with a valid choice for each instruction that
  chooses a unit (`effects.has_valid_target`: the _resolve_targets pool, moves'
  `_move_candidates`) or a spell to counter. Conditional instructions (a
  kicker's "deal 5 instead") need one of them to have a target; a card whose
  text says "up to" may have none.
- [DONE] `GameLoop.on_choice` tells every choice once (declared ones as the
  spell is played); the coach logs them and shows them on the chain.
- Approximations left: costs are paid before the choices are declared (rules:
  choose, then pay; DEFLECT's tax still goes by the spell's battlefield, not
  its target); "when you choose me" triggers fire as the spell resolves, not as
  it is played; a later instruction's choice is made on the copy after the
  earlier ones resolved (it can't name a unit the first one kills); the §355.8
  check doesn't cover gear, battlefield or trash targets, nor spells played
  facedown, by FLOW or from banishment; a move effect sends an enemy to its
  base only (Charm can't pull an enemy in from its base, §355.4);
  Resonating Strike's "a battlefield you control" is the spell's battlefield,
  unchecked. Cost: a deep copy per spell played (heuristic games 2.4x slower,
  net self-play ~8% per decision).
